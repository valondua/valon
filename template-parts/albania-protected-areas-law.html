Law 21/2024: what changed in Albania’s protected areas?

Parliament amended the 2017 protected-areas law on 22 February 2024. The changes expressly permit five-star tourism and agrotourism activities and their supporting infrastructure, weaken several specific restrictions and expand the National Territorial Council’s role. They do not abolish protected areas or automatically approve a particular resort.

Before and after: the consequential changes

Article numbers below distinguish the 2024 amending law from the provisions it changes in Law 81/2017. English descriptions are editorial translations; the linked Albanian text is authoritative.

Permitted activities · amendment Article 14 → base Article 33

Before · 2017

The 2017 law already allowed controlled activities and tourism infrastructure consistent with management plans, with prior approval and relevant National Territorial Council decisions. It was not a blanket ban on all tourism.

After · 2024

The replacement expressly allows activities in five-star-or-higher “tourism of excellence” accommodation and agrotourism accommodation, plus supporting infrastructure, even if not specified in the protected area’s declaration decision. Monitoring, restoration and environmentally permitted military activities also remain listed.

Core zones · amendment Article 20 → base Article 48

Before · 2017

The original core-zone provisions distinguished levels A and B. Level A expressly prohibited every type of construction.

After · 2024

The replacement uses a single first-degree protection regime and refers to activities allowed by Article 33 and the declaration decision. The blanket construction ban is absent. Specific bans remain, including on hunting, harmful invasive species and listed major infrastructure. “Everything is now permitted” would be inaccurate.

Who can change the rules? · amendment Article 16 → new Article 36/1

Before · 2017

The 2017 framework already involved the Council of Ministers, the environment ministry and the National Territorial Council. The latter did not first acquire a role in 2024.

After · 2024

New Article 36/1 allows changes to technical-formal declaration elements through the Council of Ministers, with territorial-council involvement in zoning. For Article 33(2), (3) and (4) activities, approval of technical development rules by the National Territorial Council itself counts as the amending act. The defined elements include zoning and permitted or prohibited activities, not merely spelling corrections.

Protection principles · amendment Articles 2–3 → base Articles 4 and 9

Before · 2017

The earlier criteria emphasised non-permission of incompatible uses and exclusion or prevention of intensive human use.

After · 2024

The amendments add adaptability and management flexibility, replace “non-permission” with “restriction” in Article 9(c), and require consideration of tourism-related economic activity and supporting infrastructure. Category changes require assessment; this is not automatic declassification after a fire.

The rest of the law: an article-by-article guide
  • Article 1 changes definitions. Articles 4–6 alter declaration procedures and repeal base Article 12(2) and Article 13. Repealing Article 13 does not abolish all zoning: amended Article 47 still provides for internal zones.
  • Articles 7–12 amend category-specific rules for national parks, natural monuments, managed reserves/nature parks, protected landscapes, marine areas and green belts. They include tourism exceptions and a larger role for territorial-council technical rules. Article 13 shifts specified municipal-park powers from the minister to the mayor.
  • Article 15 broadens who may propose a change in area boundaries; prior consultation remains in its text. Article 17 creates a framework for national-park administration offices. Article 18 provides tripartite pasture-use contracts involving the park administration, municipality and user.
  • Articles 19–22 revise internal zoning and core, sustainable-use and recreation subzones. Articles 23–25 address revenue, park-office administration and inspection powers. Article 26 sets six- and eighteen-month implementation deadlines. Article 27 provides entry into force 15 days after Official Gazette publication.

Why support it? Why oppose it?

Supporters’ stated case

The sponsors’ memorandum and committee reports argue that residents and municipalities needed income, jobs, tourism opportunities and simpler administration. Nasufi cited requests from 15 municipalities. The government proposed substantial revisions and a framework for dedicated park offices. These are recorded justifications, not proof of each MP’s private motive or of benefits actually delivered.

Critics’ documented objections

The constitutional challenge argued that the amendments weakened environmental safeguards and had procedural and consultation defects. AOS and EcoAlbania supported that challenge. Separately, the European Commission’s 2025 report assessed the 2024 amendments as a deterioration in nature protection. That assessment is distinct from a court ruling on constitutionality.

The draft is not the enacted law

The early proposal included at least 20% of a protected area under municipal administration. Do not quote that as the final law: the government and committees substantially rewrote the proposal. The enacted text instead includes municipal-park powers and pasture-contract provisions. Committee records describe consultation in December 2023 and hearings in January–February 2024; the dispute includes whether the substantially changed text received adequate consultation, not simply whether any meetings occurred.

The constitutional case: what the outcome establishes

Decision 45 of 31 July 2025, announced on 31 October 2025, rejected the challenge brought by 37 MPs represented by Ina Zhupa. AOS and EcoAlbania participated in support. The judgment includes dissent and issues on which the required five-judge majority was not reached. Rejection did not repeal the law; it is not certification that every future project is lawful or that the amendments satisfy every EU environmental requirement.

How this connects to the coastal investigation

The law changes the legal framework relevant to projects in protected areas. It does not name Jared Kushner, transfer Sazan or Zvërnec, establish ownership, or supply a project’s environmental and construction permissions. For any particular project, the location, protected-area boundaries, declaration decision, permits and land records must be checked separately. A general legal change is not proof that a named person caused it.
