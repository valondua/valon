Historical evidence, separate from political rhetoric

Late antiquity · Physical evidence, not an arrival date

Sarandë’s synagogue is physical evidence of a Jewish community in present-day Albania. Hebrew University’s excavation release dates it to the fifth or sixth century CE. This does not establish the first arrival of Jews, nor the identity of inhabitants in earlier Roman villa layers. First-century shipwreck accounts are traditions, not established archaeology.

Read the sources: University excavation release · Academic review

1492–1550 · Several migrations to Ottoman Vlorë

Nadia Zeldes and David Ben Zazon’s 2025 study uses rabbinic and archival sources to trace successive migrations from Iberia and southern Italy after expulsions. It documents different congregations and communities, rather than a single arrival story. No unverified percentage for Vlorë’s Jewish population is adopted here.

Read the sources: 2025 academic study

Holocaust rescue · Documented help and its limits

Albanian families sheltered local Jews and refugees. Absolute claims that nobody was arrested or deported go beyond the evidence: Yad Vashem researchers record the Arditi family’s arrest in Shkodër in October 1944 and deportation to Prishtina, with family members killed. Albania within its prewar borders must be distinguished from occupied or annexed Kosovo.

Read the sources: Yad Vashem research, pp. 5–6 · Mandil and Veseli family record

What remains to be established

The documented connections are those named in the speeches and project records above. Identity, awards and museum cooperation alone do not prove territorial transfer, resettlement, criminality or coordinated religious control. The next useful records are the Elton facsimile; actual hearing records and the signed municipal reply; heritage assessments and permits; and award contracts, variations and payments.
