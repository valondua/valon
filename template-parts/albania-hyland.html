Biography · attributed

1992 onward · A campaign role, not a current government mandate

Hyland’s biography identifies him as deputy national political director for ethnic constituencies in Bill Clinton’s 1992 campaign. His later advocacy links American diaspora networks with Albanian affairs. That campaign title does not make him a current U.S. government envoy.

Public advocacy · firsthand interview

25 March 2025 · The Bektashi campaign

In an Albanian Daily News interview, Hyland supports the Bektashi proposal and names Baba Mondi, Hazis Vardar and himself as working toward it. His website describes him as the Bektashi headquarters’ special envoy to North America. This is a representative role for that institution, not evidence of a U.S. appointment or recognised statehood.

Hyland’s description of his mandate

Archived self-description · historical advocacy verified

By 17 August 2001 · An Orthodox church-state idea

An archived biography on Hyland’s company website says he championed “the creation of a Sovereign Orthodox Church State within Kosovo”. The same passage also supports Kosovo’s independence and a stable, democratic Serbia.

17 August 2001 is the archive capture date, not a verified date of authorship or submission. The wording also appears in captures from 10 June 2019 and 27 February 2024. This verifies historical advocacy in his own account; it does not establish an adopted plan, recognised state or government mandate.

Retrospective account · not independently corroborated

13 October 2020 · Recalling the 1992 discussions

Ruben Avxhiu’s Illyria account of an interview with Hyland describes Jim Xhema introducing him to Ibrahim Rugova, discussions of religious-site autonomy, Rugova’s reported openness and resistance from George Stephanopoulos. It says Hyland never secured Clinton campaign approval.

We verified the published account, not the private discussions. It supplies no signed plan or endorsement. Autonomy is not necessarily sovereignty; this account does not establish the exact legal model behind the archived church-state wording.
